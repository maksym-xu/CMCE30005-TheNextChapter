# Evidence map for the final presentation

This map connects the ten presentation slides to the published aggregate evidence and explains what each check establishes. Paths below are relative to the repository's `final-presentation/` directory. The presentation and speaking document are in `deliverables/`; the editable slide source and synchronized speaking text are in `code/build_deck.mjs` and `code/script.json`.

The historical project source is commit `7ea295c50999472bc91b5f247e75a187b4171545`. Later model, presentation and supplementary-check files are extensions of that source, not evidence that the historical commit contained them. The repository's Git history identifies the publication revision.

## What can be verified from this public package

There are different levels of evidence. They must not be described as one independent, complete raw-data validation.

1. **Existing project pipeline and frozen source evidence.** The original project reconstructed review counts from dated school-supplied reviews, applied the eligibility and host-partition rules, and recorded source hashes. Descriptive uncertainty intervals and the two-window historical check are preserved in `analysis/reference/`. Their published tables and provenance can be inspected here; their original raw-data reconstruction and bootstrap runs are not rerun by a public aggregate check.
2. **Model computations using authorised local inputs.** The simple logistic model, group-rate comparator, association checks and matched-input forest have executable source under `analysis/scripts/`. Their saved outputs came from the prepared school-data input and existing host folds. Rebuilding them requires the authorised source/processed data described in `analysis/README.md`. The public package does not include that private input or listing-level out-of-fold predictions.
3. **Supplementary calculations from frozen predictions.** The three-recommended-group checks reuse existing out-of-fold predictions without refitting a model. Python calculations and a separate R implementation check the quota arithmetic and resulting counts. Their agreement verifies those calculations on the frozen input; it does not create new observations, remove earlier selection decisions or independently validate the complete modelling pipeline.
4. **Public aggregate checks.** `analysis/scripts/check_publication.py` uses public tables to check arithmetic and agreement across files. It can check denominators, rounded percentages, quotas, reported coefficient directions and the identity of the temporal candidate groups. It cannot recreate individual predictions, prove host separation, audit raw review dates, re-estimate coefficients, or regenerate confidence intervals without the non-public inputs. Follow `analysis/README.md` for the separate public-check and authorised-data reproduction routes.

Published shares and probabilities generally use fractions from 0 to 1. Fields ending in `_pp` are percentage-point differences; explicitly named percentage/display fields are already percentages. Slide labels round to one decimal place. Small floating-point differences around an integer quota are numerical precision, not extra listings.

## Supplementary evidence for questions

### Two model associations

Use `analysis/outputs/association_stability.json` and `association_stability_summary.csv` for the qualitative slide-5 claims. Terms are `one_nightYes` and `price_vs_similar_10`. Their training-fold coefficient ranges are respectively 1.067–1.343 and −0.141 to −0.119; all five reported host-clustered intervals exclude zero in the stated direction.

The relative-price unit is **ten percentage points above the same area/type/bedroom group's median price**, not AUD10 or a ten-dollar change. Full-data odds ratios of approximately 3.196 and 0.880 are technical backup, not risk ratios or causal percentage gains. Longer stays can generate fewer separate reviews. Current settings may have changed or responded to historical activity. The source is `analysis/scripts/association_stability.R`; public checks only reconcile its published results.

### Why logistic regression is presented after an interim forest

`analysis/reference/interim-model-selection/` preserves the earlier model-selection record. The interim enhanced property-only forest had AUC about 0.640 and Brier score 0.1813; the same-input logistic comparison had approximately 0.607 and 0.1869. Comparing that old forest with the new logistic AUC of 0.705 would mix a change of inputs with a change of algorithm.

The new matched-input benchmark is in `analysis/outputs/matched_rf_comparison.csv`, `.json` and `_intervals.csv`, produced by `analysis/scripts/matched_rf_comparison.R`. It uses the same six inputs, host tests and expected quota of 969. Forest screening precision is 45.15%, versus 43.65% for logistic; the difference is +1.49 percentage points with a conditional host-bootstrap range of −2.35 to +7.31 points. Logistic has better point estimates for AUC and Brier score in this comparison. Neither family is established as universally best.

The new fixed R forest is not a recreation of the interim Python forest. Its resampling intervals hold predictions fixed and omit refitting and tuning. Logistic is retained for explanation and screening; publication does not establish individual probability calibration. See `analysis/outputs/calibration.csv` for observed rates versus score bins.

### Checks inside the three recommended groups

These support Q&A and do **not** replace slide 6's full-sample result. Sources are `analysis/outputs/shortlisted_group_validation.json`, `.csv`, `_by_group.csv`, `_group_fold_cells.csv`, `_group_fold_summary.csv`, `_fold_diagnostic.csv` and `_intervals.csv`. Calculation code is `analysis/scripts/shortlisted_group_validation.py`; the separate numerical implementation is `analysis/scripts/verify_shortlisted_group_validation.R`.

The three groups contain 1,631 listings, 625 distinct hosts and 544 historical target attainments. Distinct-host counts by group overlap and must not be added to claim a total.

| Frozen-prediction check | Quota and published result | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| Pool three groups, then rank | 408 places; logistic 201/408 = 49.26%; stored cross-fold group mean 22.02%. | Diagnostic only. The low comparator should not be presented as a commercial advantage. |
| Fix a quota in each group | 309, 77 and 23 places, total 409; logistic 202/409 = 49.39%; uniform within-group selection expects 136.415/409 = 33.353%. | Exploratory evidence that historical ranking signal remains inside the chosen groups. Difference is +16.04 points. It is not an observed randomised business trial. |
| Fix a quota in every group × held-out-fold cell | 15 cells and 416 places; logistic 222/416 = 53.365%; within-cell random expectation 138.700/416 = 33.341%. | Sensitivity that prevents allocation changes between groups or folds. Extra places arise from separate rounding; this is a different rule, not a new test set or deployable optimum. No new interval was calculated for this sensitivity. |

The stored training-fold group-mean score is constant within a group-and-fold cell but varies across folds. Pooling these scores inside the recommended groups can rank the omitted folds unfavourably. The fixed-group stored-baseline result of 19.92% is therefore also a diagnostic, not the neutral comparison. This issue does not by itself demonstrate test-host leakage.

For the fixed-group rule, the 1,000-host-resample conditional 95% range is 33.63–65.46% for model precision and +3.62 to +27.83 points for its difference from uniform within-group selection. Predictions and the chosen groups remain fixed; quotas, tie weights and the reference rate are recalculated within each resample. These ranges omit model refitting, earlier model choices and selection of the three groups. The check is post-exploration and does not establish new-operator or future performance.

## Date and provenance boundaries

The school labels the source as the June 2026 Inside Airbnb dataset; recorded listing scrape dates run from 17 June to 1 July 2026. For each listing's scrape date S, the primary target uses `(S − 365 days, S]`. The temporal check reconstructs `(S − 730 days, S − 365 days]` and `(S − 365 days, S]` from the same dated review file. It does not use four quarterly snapshots. The temporal provenance records the same raw-file hashes as the primary project evidence. Hash agreement identifies files; it is not an independent audit of data collection or completeness.

The temporal analysis changes eligibility to require the earlier review history, leaving 2,657 primary-sample listings. Its 34-review threshold and denominators must not be substituted into the 3,873-listing, 30-review model analysis. The 27.1% and 14.9% bars concern the later window only. Any overall decline concerns this surviving cohort, not a finding that every group or the whole Melbourne market declined.

## Publication and reproduction boundary

The public release contains aggregate tables, analysis source, documented references and presentation sources/deliverables. It excludes raw school files, the compact listing-level input, listing/host identifiers, individual predictions, private working files and machine-specific absolute paths. Sanitised provenance may retain source-file hashes and repository-relative names.

An `analysis/scripts/check_publication.py` pass means that the distributed aggregate evidence is internally consistent under the implemented checks. It must not be described as independently reproducing the data cleaning, model training, leakage checks, raw review-window reconstruction or confidence intervals. Authorised-data reproduction is a separate workflow. PPT rendering/package checks confirm artifact structure and displayed content; native animation playback, when checked separately, does not validate the analytical results.
